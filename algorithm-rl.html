Reinforcement Learning

No population, no generations — just one agent, learning gradually from whatever happens each time it drops a piece.

Learning while playing

The update, worked out

Say the agent guessed a placement was worth +6. It plays it, gets no line clear, and the resulting board is only worth +3 by its own current judgment. The gap between what it expected and what it got — the TD error — is 3 − 6 = −3. Every feature that was large in the placement it just made (holes, height, …) gets nudged a little in the direction that would have predicted −3 instead of +6.

No lookahead, no population — just one number, corrected a little after every single move.

⚡ Act

Usually play the best-looking move by current weights; occasionally try a random one.

→
🏆 Reward

Surviving the move and clearing lines both count as reward.

→
≈ Compare

Was the new board as good as expected? The gap is the TD error.

→
⏱ Remember

Keep a fading record of recent moves, so a surprise can blame them too.

→
✎ Adjust

Nudge every remembered move toward what would have predicted correctly.

That fourth step is the eligibility trace. Without it, a nasty surprise only corrects the single move right before it — but the placement that actually doomed you was often made much earlier. The trace keeps every recent move partially "on the hook", fading with age, so one surprise corrects all of them at once. On the classic random-walk benchmark it cuts prediction error by about 20%.

Why it still lags behind

Even with traces it plateaus well below the other three, and the reason is more interesting than "needs more training". Look at the weights it actually settles on:

FeatureWhat a strong player wantsWhat RL actually learned
Holes avoid strongly barely cares (≈ 0)
Max height avoid seeks it
Relative height avoid seeks it, strongly

It concluded that taller and more uneven is better — and by its own objective, it isn't wrong. It gets rewarded for surviving, so it is trained to predict how much reward is still coming, and a tall board genuinely does mean "I have already survived a while". The correlation is real.

But a rule that predicts well is not the same as a rule that plays well — and this agent picks its moves with the predictor. That gap is the whole difficulty. GA, CMA-ES and NEAT never face it: they're scored directly on the thing you actually care about, the final game score, so nothing ever rewards them for merely correlating with survival.
